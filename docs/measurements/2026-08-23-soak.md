# The soak run

**Date:** 2026-08-23 / 24
**Increment:** M1 / 2 (transport), task 13
**Harness:** `cmd/avitosoak`, one JSONL record per request
**Query:** `https://m.avito.ru/all?q=iphone+15&s=104`, mobile surface
**Egress:** one address throughout — this machine's own
**Raw data:** `soak-data/*.jsonl.gz`, with the analyser that produced every table here

Every throughput figure in the design was extrapolated from forty requests. This
run replaced them with measurements, and in doing so refuted four things the
design believed and two things this document said in an earlier draft.

> **Read §8 before quoting any number.** Two arms are confounded and one was
> recorded by an instrument that could not see refusals.

---

## §1 The arms

Direct first throughout, so BlankTrail always worked from an address the direct
arms had already annoyed — the discipline `Avito_Parser/docs/blanktrail/
RESULTS.md` used, which exists to make the comparison harder for the paid mode
rather than easier. The one exception is `blanktrail-exhaust`, which was given a
measured rest first (§7); that is stated wherever its numbers appear.

| arm | transport | endpoints | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| `direct-1jar` | utls, no proxy | 1 jar | first look |
| `direct-4jars` | utls, no proxy | 4 jars, one address | question 2 |
| `blanktrail-2ports` | BlankTrail | 2 ports | first look |
| `direct-norenew` | utls, no proxy | 1 jar, renewal disabled | separate the wait from the renewal |
| `direct-exhaust` | utls, no proxy | 1 jar | run until it stops |
| `blanktrail-exhaust` | BlankTrail | 2 ports | run until it stops, from a rested address |
| `catch429` | utls, no proxy | 4 jars | provoke a 429 and keep its body |

The ceiling was lifted on every arm. The shipped default — 60 requests per
10 minutes per address — is *stricter* than the 3.5 s floor, which allows 17 per
minute, so leaving it in place would have measured our own safety limit instead
of the site's tolerance. **The shipped budget is therefore still untested as
shipped**; see §9.

## §2 What each arm did

| arm | fetches | clean | refusals | first | ids | wall | in remedy | clean/hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `direct-1jar` | 100 | 100 | *not recorded* | — | 2 | 10.3 m | — | 581 |
| `direct-4jars` | 82 | 71 | 14 | 1 | 6 | 185.4 m | 183.6 m | **23** |
| `blanktrail-2ports` | 100 | 100 | 1 | 47 | 3 | 13.0 m | 6.0 m | 462 |
| `direct-norenew` | 100 | 100 | 2 | 94 | **1** | 30.4 m | 24.2 m | 198 |
| `direct-exhaust` | 391 | 391 | 10 | 53 | 11 | 93.1 m | 68.9 m | 252 |
| `blanktrail-exhaust` | 392 | 389 | 10 | 93 | 12 | 145.2 m | 119.5 m | 161 |
| `catch429` | 39 | 38 | 1 | 39 | 4 | 0.7 m | 0 | — |

"Clean" is a fetch that ended in `ok` or `empty`. "Refusal" is a fetch in which
the site said no at least once, whatever the outcome — the two differ, and only
the second is a rate of being pushed back.

## §3 Question 1: does a warm session hold at 3.5 s, and for how long?

**Yes, and there is no ceiling in requests.**

`direct-exhaust` ran 391 fetches from one address. All 391 came back clean. Ten
were refused first and recovered:

```
refusals at   53  76  185  186  284  285  304  305  379  391
gaps          53  23  109    1   98    1   19    1   74   12
clean runs    52  22  108   97   18   73   11
```

No degradation — the gap after the eighth refusal (74) is longer than the gap
before it (19). The arm was stopped by the operator on a steady state, not by
the site: at that rate the 5000-fetch cap was nineteen hours away and would not
have said anything new.

**What direct mode costs is time, not requests.** Sixty-nine of its ninety-three
minutes — **74%** — went on waiting out ten refusals. That is not the shape
"300–700 requests" suggests.

## §4 Question 2: do several warm jars on one address beat one?

**No. They are worse by an order of magnitude.**

| | one jar | four jars, one address |
|---|---|---|
| clean/hour | 252 | **23** |
| refusals | 10 in 391 (2.6%) | 14 in 82 (17%) |
| time in remedy | 74% | **99%** |

The 429 is bound to the **address**. Four jars reach its limit sooner and then
all four are refused at once, and with no upstream to move to the ladder spent
887 seconds per fetch — six attempts up a backoff ladder — achieving nothing,
eleven times running.

**A jar pool is only worth having with one address per jar.** This is the
measurement behind the requirement that N ports each carry their own IP: without
it the pool is not neutral, it is harmful.

The arm also exposed a bug that made it worse than it needed to be. `Pacer.Wait`
takes an identity and an address, and the ladder was passing `Lease.Label` as
the address — which names the *endpoint*, not the exit. Four jars on one IP were
therefore granted four separate ten-minute windows and four times the budget
they were entitled to. `Budget()` reported one address throughout, which is what
kept it invisible: the published figure was right and the pacing under it was
not. Fixed; `Lease.Egress` is now the address and endpoints sharing one return
the same string.

## §5 The 429 is the captcha, and the 403 is not

This is the run's central finding and it reverses the remedy table.

| | cleared by waiting? |
|---|---|
| **403** | **18 of 18.** None failed |
| **429** | **0 of 66.** None succeeded |

Sixty-six consecutive 429s went through the old backoff ladder — eleven fetches,
about fifteen minutes apiece — and not one became a 200. The only two that
recovered did so through a **302**, which is how a challenge flow ends rather
than how a rate limit expires.

`catch429` then provoked one and kept the body. It is
`internal/transport/testdata/live-429-captcha.html.gz`, 27 KB, titled «Доступ
ограничен: проблема с IP», and it contains `geetest`, `firewallCaptcha`,
`initGeetest` and `x-cube`. **It is a Geetest page.**

The classifier had been calling it a rate limit for one reason: `Document.Wall`
returns markers in table order, «проблема с ip» is listed before «geetest», and
the captcha test looked at that single returned label. `Document.Walls` now
reports every marker and the classifier scans all of them. The cold check still
runs first, deliberately — an identity that has never warmed up is expected to
be refused, and reading that as a challenge would leave direct mode unable to
start.

**What changed in the code**

- `ClassBurned` → `RemedyWait`. Renewing discarded a Qrator clearance worth
  19–23 s to fix something the identity was not causing. Renewal survives only
  as a fallback past three waits, where the measurement stops speaking.
- `ClassThrottled` → rotate, one turn per configured address, then **halt** with
  a boundary message naming `-proxies` and BlankTrail. Verified live: the halt
  arrives in 40 seconds where the old ladder ground for hours.
- The backoff ladder is deleted. Its absence is the finding.

## §6 A soft block is a stuck endpoint

`blanktrail-exhaust` ended by refuting the one remedy left running on an
assumption. Three fetches exhausted all six attempts on an empty 200 — six
identity renewals apiece, twenty-seven minutes apiece:

```
387  soft-block  1610s  20000#10     389  soft-block  1601s  20000#16
388  ok             1s  20011#2      390  ok             2s  20011#2
391  soft-block  1660s  20000#22     392  ok             1s  20011#2
```

Every failure on port 20000; port 20011, **on the same exit address**, answered
in under two seconds throughout. Over the whole arm:

| port | fetches | with a refusal | total time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20000 | 196 | 8 | **127 min** |
| 20011 | 196 | 2 | **18 min** |

`Lease.RenewIdentity` rotates the profile; the port, and whatever the proxy
holds for it, survives that. Six renewals inside one fetch could not have helped
and did not. Waiting could not either — those fetches waited four minutes
between attempts and were still handed an empty page twenty-seven minutes later.

`Lease.Recycle` closes the endpoint and opens a new one, keeping the address.
The vendored SDK already had the close-and-reopen inside `renewIfDue`, reachable
only on a schedule or after a failed renewal; it is lifted out as
`Pool.recycle`. Direct mode has no port, so its recycle is a new fingerprint
with a new jar and new connections. A soft block now costs about ten seconds
instead of twenty-seven minutes, with two recycles as the limit — past that the
endpoint is not what is wrong.

## §7 Address recovery

The first number this project has for how long a refused address stays refused.

| | |
|---|---|
| `direct-exhaust` stopped | 18:32:59 |
| probe 1, +15 min | `class=ok`, status 200, **first attempt** |
| probe 2, +30 min | `class=ok`, status 200, **first attempt** |

Each probe was one request on a **fresh throwaway identity**, so what recovered
is the address and not an accumulated jar. It also incidentally confirms the
cold-start path: a brand-new identity got a clean 200 without passing through
`ClassCold`.

Fifteen minutes is an upper bound — the address may have cleared sooner, and
nothing here narrows it further. It is the same order as the budget's ten-minute
window, which is reassuring about that choice without confirming it.

## §8 What is wrong with these numbers

Stated plainly, because most of the arms have a defect a reader would otherwise
not see.

1. **`direct-1jar` was recorded blind.** It ran before `Result.Trace` existed,
   so its zero refusals is the absence of an instrument, not a measurement. It
   had a refusal at request 57, visible only as 244 seconds and a changed
   session. **No comparison here may use its refusal count.**

2. **`direct-4jars` started hot.** It began straight after `direct-1jar`'s 100
   requests and its very first fetch was already `403 -> 403 -> 403 -> ok`. Its
   clean/hour figure stands; its "29 requests before the first throttle"
   compares to nothing.

3. **`blanktrail-exhaust` started rested and `direct-exhaust` did not.** The
   direct arm began on an address `direct-norenew` had just used 100 times. That
   confound runs in BlankTrail's favour, so §9's gap is an upper bound on its
   advantage rather than a lower one.

4. **Every arm shares one egress address and they ran back to back.** Each
   inherits what the last one left.

5. **One run per condition.** Nothing here is a distribution.

6. **The captcha inference is timing, not observation.** BlankTrail solves
   silently, so the client cannot see a solve; §9's bimodality is consistent
   with silent solving and does not prove it.

7. **`go test -race` does not run on this machine** — the race detector needs
   cgo and there is no gcc on PATH. The suite is green without it; `-race` is
   CI's job.

## §9 BlankTrail against direct, on equal samples

Both exhaustion arms stopped at 391 and 392 fetches. The comparison splits in
two, because averaging a working phase with a collapse describes neither.

**Before port 20000 stuck:**

| first 386 fetches | direct | blanktrail |
|---|---|---|
| clean | 386 | 386 |
| refusals | 9 | **7** |
| wall | 88.8 min | **64.0 min** |
| time waiting | 73% | **60%** |
| **clean/hour** | 261 | **362** |

**Whole arm**, including the collapse: direct 252 clean/hour, BlankTrail 161.
That inversion is entirely the three stuck fetches — 81 of BlankTrail's
119 minutes of remedy — and it is a bug in the remedy table (§6), now fixed,
rather than a property of the transport.

**Where the difference comes from.** Single-attempt clean fetches, elapsed:

```
direct       3-5 s: 94%          one outlier at 21 s      unimodal
blanktrail   0-2 s: 49%   5-7 s: 34%   7-10 s: 15%        bimodal, nothing between 3 and 5
```

Two populations with a gap, not a spread. Per port: 20000 had 81% of its fetches
over 5 s, 20011 only 19%. That is the silent solving — the proxy answers a
challenge and the client waits about five seconds for a clean page.

Direct met **no** challenges in 391 fetches and BlankTrail met none it showed the
client. What direct met instead was ten 403s at about 413 seconds each. So the
two transports do not differ in how far they get before the site pushes back —
53 and 93, the same order — they differ in what the push-back costs.

**About 1.4× the throughput**, on a comparison tilted in BlankTrail's favour by
§8.3. Not "multiples more requests"; the same requests, materially cheaper.

## §10 The number that replaces "300–700"

The claim was the wrong shape in both directions, and nothing measured here
supports restating it as a request count.

- Direct mode has **no measured ceiling in requests**. It ran 391 without one
  and was stopped by the clock.
- What it has is a **74% time tax** — ten refusals at about seven minutes each.
- BlankTrail pays the same 403s and about the same intervals, but absorbs
  challenges at five seconds instead of meeting them as blocks.

**The honest figures, one address, mobile search, 3.5 s floor, ceiling lifted:**

| | clean requests/hour |
|---|---|
| direct (utls, no proxy) | **250–260** |
| BlankTrail (2 ports) | **360** |

Both were stopped by the operator, not by the site. Both carry §8's confounds.
Neither is a ceiling. Any README line built on these must say the sample size
and that the arms shared one address, or it repeats the sin "300–700" committed.

**Still not measured:**

- The shipped budget as shipped. Every arm ran with the ceiling lifted.
  `direct-exhaust` sustained about 42 requests per 10 minutes against a default
  of 60, and met a refusal every 39 fetches. Close enough that the default is
  plausible and far from demonstrated.
- Anything with a real proxy pool. Every arm used one address, which is the one
  configuration §4 says not to run.
- Whether the 15-minute recovery is a floor or a plateau.
