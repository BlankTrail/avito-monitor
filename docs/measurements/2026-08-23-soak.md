# The soak run

**Date:** 2026-08-23
**Increment:** M1 / 2 (transport), task 13
**Harness:** `cmd/avitosoak`, JSONL per request
**Query:** `https://m.avito.ru/all?q=iphone+15&s=104`, mobile surface
**Egress:** one address, shared by every arm — this machine's own

This run exists because every throughput figure in the design was extrapolated
from forty requests. Three questions had no measured answer. Two of them now do,
one of them contradicts what the design assumed, and the run also refuted a
claim this document's own author made about the first arm.

> **Read §7 before quoting any number from §2.** Two arms are confounded and one
> was recorded by an instrument that could not see refusals.

---

## §1 The arms

Direct first throughout, so BlankTrail always worked from an address the direct
arms had already annoyed. That is the discipline `Avito_Parser/docs/blanktrail/
RESULTS.md` used and it exists to make the comparison harder for the paid mode,
not easier.

| arm | transport | jars/ports | spacing | ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `direct-1jar` | utls, no proxy | 1 | 3.5 s | lifted |
| `direct-4jars` | utls, no proxy | 4, one address | 3.5 s/jar | lifted |
| `blanktrail-2ports` | BlankTrail | 2 ports | 3.5 s | lifted |
| `direct-norenew` | utls, no proxy | 1, **renewal disabled** | 3.5 s | lifted |
| `direct-exhaust` | utls, no proxy | 1 | 3.5 s | lifted |
| `blanktrail-exhaust` | BlankTrail | 2 ports | 3.5 s | lifted |

The ceiling was lifted deliberately. The shipped default — 60 requests per
10 minutes per address — is *stricter* than the 3.5 s floor, which allows 17 per
minute. Left in place, every arm would have measured our own safety limit rather
than the site's tolerance. **The shipped budget is therefore still untested as
shipped**; see §6.

## §2 What each arm did

| arm | fetches | clean | refusals | first | ids | wall | in remedy | clean/hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `direct-1jar` | 100 | 100 | *not recorded* | — | 2 | 10.3 m | — | 581 |
| `direct-4jars` | 82 | 71 | 14 | 1 | 6 | 185.4 m | 183.6 m | **23** |
| `blanktrail-2ports` | 100 | 100 | 1 | 47 | 3 | 13.0 m | 6.0 m | 462 |
| `direct-norenew` | 100 | 100 | 2 | 94 | **1** | 30.4 m | 24.2 m | 198 |
| `direct-exhaust` | 391 | 391 | 10 | 53 | 11 | 93.1 m | 68.9 m | 252 |

"Clean" is a fetch that ended in `ok` or `empty`. "Refusal" is a fetch in which
the site said no at least once, whatever the outcome — the two differ, and only
the second is a rate of being pushed back.

## §3 Question 1: does a warm session hold at 3.5 s, and for how long?

**Yes, and there is no ceiling in requests.**

`direct-exhaust` ran 391 fetches from one address. All 391 came back clean. Ten
of them were refused first and recovered:

```
refusals at   53  76  185  186  284  285  304  305  379  391
gaps          53  23  109    1   98    1   19    1   74   12
clean runs    52  22  108   97   18   73   11
```

No degradation. The gap after the eighth refusal (74) is longer than the gap
before it (19). The arm was stopped by the operator on a steady state, not by
the site: at this rate the 5000-fetch cap was nineteen hours away and would not
have said anything new.

**What direct mode actually costs is time, not requests.** Sixty-nine of its
ninety-three minutes — **74%** — were spent waiting out ten refusals. That is
the honest shape of the constraint, and it is not the shape "300–700 requests"
suggests.

## §4 Question 2: do several warm jars on one address beat one?

**No. They are worse by an order of magnitude, and the design assumed otherwise.**

| | one jar | four jars, one address |
|---|---|---|
| clean/hour | 252 | **23** |
| refusals | 10 in 391 (2.6%) | 14 in 82 (17%) |
| time in remedy | 74% | **99%** |

The 429 is bound to the **address**. Four jars reach the address's limit sooner
and then all four are throttled at once, and with no upstream to move to the
ladder spent 887 seconds per fetch — six attempts up the backoff ladder —
achieving nothing, eleven times in a row.

**A jar pool is only worth having with one address per jar.** This is the
measurement behind the requirement that N ports each carry their own IP: without
it the pool is not neutral, it is harmful.

## §5 Question 3, and the correction that matters most

The first write-up of `direct-1jar` said its request 57 recovered *because the
ladder renewed the identity*. That was wrong. The ladder renews the identity
**and** waits four minutes, and the arm could not tell the two apart.

`direct-norenew` separates them. `RenewIdentity` was replaced with a no-op, so a
block was met with the wait alone:

```
one session across all 100 fetches — the srv_id never changed
n=94   965 s   403 -> 403 -> 403 -> 403 -> ok
n=95   484 s   403 -> 403 -> ok
```

**A 403 heals with time alone.** Recovery tracks the number of 240-second waits
and nothing else. `direct-exhaust` confirms it from the other side: with renewal
enabled, request 76 still needed `403 -> 403 -> 403 -> ok` and 724 seconds —
three renewals bought nothing.

The identity is not what the site refused. **The address is.**

Consistent with that, the arm that never renewed went further before its first
refusal (94) than the arms that did (53, 57). One observation each, so that
comparison is suggestive rather than settled — but the mechanism is not in
doubt, because recovery happened with no renewal at all.

### What this changes in the code

`Class.Remedy()` maps `ClassBurned` to `RemedyRenewIdentity`. That is refuted:
renewing discards a Qrator clearance worth 19–23 seconds to fix something the
identity was not causing. A 403 wants the same treatment as a throttle — wait,
and rotate the egress if there is one. The test asserting that
`RemedyRotateEgress` is reachable only from `ClassThrottled` encodes the refuted
belief and goes with it.

**Not measured:** whether `ClassSoftBlock` — a 200 with no container — behaves
the same way. It is a different signal and keeps its own remedy until someone
measures it.

## §6 Things this run found that nobody was looking for

**A 302 that no measurement describes.** Two fetches recorded
`throttled/429 -> soft-block/302 -> ok/200`. A 302 reaches the classifier only
when Go declines to follow it, which means its `Location` was missing or
unusable. There is **no 3xx branch in the classifier at all**: the redirect body
failed extraction and landed in `ClassSoftBlock` through the `ErrNoExtractor`
path, whose remedy is the renewal §5 just refuted. The ladder now keeps the body
for any 3xx and for `ClassOther` so the next one is diagnosable. No branch was
added — there is nothing to read yet.

**The shipped budget is still untested.** Every arm ran with the ceiling lifted.
`DefaultCeiling` is 60 per 10 minutes; `direct-exhaust` sustained about 250 per
hour, or 42 per 10 minutes, and met a refusal every 39 fetches on average. That
is close enough to the default that the default is plausible and far from
demonstrated. It needs its own arm, run as shipped.

**Recovery time had never been measured.** The probe series in §8 is the first
number for it, and the whole two-level budget rests on an assumption about it.

## §7 What is wrong with these numbers

Stated plainly, because three of the five arms have a defect a reader would
otherwise not see.

1. **`direct-1jar` was recorded blind.** It ran before `Result.Trace` existed,
   so its zero refusals is the absence of an instrument, not a measurement. It
   had a refusal at request 57 — visible only as 244 seconds and a changed
   session. **No comparison in this document may use its refusal count.**

2. **`direct-4jars` started hot.** It began immediately after `direct-1jar`'s
   100 requests, and its very first fetch was already
   `403 -> 403 -> 403 -> ok`. Its clean/hour figure stands. Its "29 requests
   before the first throttle" compares to nothing.

3. **Every arm shares one egress address, and they ran back to back.** Each arm
   inherits the state the previous one left. The direct-first ordering makes
   BlankTrail's numbers a lower bound rather than an inflated one, which is the
   right direction for a document that exists partly to demonstrate BlankTrail —
   but it is a confound and not a control.

4. **One run per condition.** Nothing here is a distribution.

5. **`go test -race` does not run on this machine** — the race detector needs
   cgo and there is no gcc on PATH. The suite is green without it; `-race` is
   CI's job.
